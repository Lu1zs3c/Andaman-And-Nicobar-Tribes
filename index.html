Two Distinct Ancestral Roots

The indigenous peoples of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands are categorized into two primary ethnic groups based on geographical separation, genetics, and distinct cultural histories.

Andaman Islands (Negrito Descent)

Believed to have migrated from Africa tens of thousands of years ago. These hunter-gatherer tribes are characterized by dark skin, short stature, and deep connection to tropical forests and marine foraging.

Great Andamanese Onge Jarawa Sentinelese

Nicobar Islands (Mongoloid Descent)

Sharing closer ethnic and linguistic ties with Southeast Asian populations. These communities practice horticulture, animal husbandry, and coastal fishing, with distinct societal structures.

PVTG Status Strait Island

Great Andamanese

Negrito Descent • Once 10 sub-tribes

Once consisting of 10 distinct sub-tribes, numbers were drastically reduced due to colonial diseases and conflict. The surviving population has been resettled on Strait Island.

Lifestyle: Settled / Community
PVTG Status Little Andaman

Onge Tribe

Negrito Descent • Semi-Nomadic

Semi-nomadic hunter-gatherers traditionally dependent on forest and coastal marine resources. Currently settled in two reserves at Dugong Creek and South Bay.

Lifestyle: Hunter-Gatherer
PVTG Status South & Middle Andaman

Jarawa Tribe

Negrito Descent • Forest Nomads

Inhabit the dense western forest reserves of South and Middle Andaman. They maintained strict isolation until the late 1990s and now have carefully monitored regulated interactions.

Lifestyle: Nomadic Foragers
Uncontacted Tribe North Sentinel Island

Sentinelese

Negrito Descent • Fully Uncontacted

One of the world's last fully uncontacted peoples. They live in absolute isolation on North Sentinel Island and fiercely resist any outside intrusion to protect their autonomy.

Lifestyle: Sovereign Hunter-Gatherers
Scheduled Tribe Car Nicobar & Others

Nicobarese Tribe

Mongoloid Descent • Horticultural Economy

The largest and most populous indigenous group across the Nicobar archipelago. They engage in coconut cultivation, pig herding, and have a vibrant community life with local governance.

Lifestyle: Horticultural & Coastal
PVTG Status Great Nicobar Island

Shompen Tribe

Mongoloid Descent • Deep Interior Foragers

A semi-nomadic, deeply isolated tribe residing in the dense interior biosphere of Great Nicobar Island. They rely on foraging, hunting wild pigs, and primitive yam cultivation.

Lifestyle: Semi-Nomadic Foragers

How to Host This Site on GitHub Pages

Follow these 4 simple steps to deploy this exact webpage live on the internet using GitHub.

1

Create a New Repository

Go to github.com, log into your account, and click the green "New" button to create a new public repository (e.g., named andaman-tribes).

2

Save File as index.html

Copy all the code from this single file and save it locally on your computer with the exact filename: index.html.

3

Upload to GitHub

Inside your new repository, click "uploading an existing file", drag and drop your index.html file into the browser window, and commit the changes.

4

Enable GitHub Pages

Go to your repository Settings > Pages. Under "Branch", select main (or master) and root / (root), then click Save!
